# Growth, Reinvestment and Terminal Economics

## Fundamental growth
When margins and capital efficiency are stable:
- Equity growth: `g_EPS = ROE × Retention Ratio`
- Operating-income growth: `g_EBIT = ROC × Reinvestment Rate`

These are consistency equations, not forecasts by themselves.

## Reinvestment
A company cannot receive growth without paying for it.

Two useful formulations:
- `Reinvestment Rate = Reinvestment / NOPAT`
- `Reinvestment = Change in Revenue / Sales-to-Capital Ratio`

Higher sales-to-capital means greater investment efficiency. Lower sales-to-capital means greater capital intensity.

## Growth from changing economics
If margins, capital efficiency or business mix are changing, forecast those drivers directly. Do not force `g = ROC × reinvestment` on a transition period where the economics are not stable.

## Competitive advantage period
The high-growth period represents how long a firm can maintain returns/growth above mature-economy norms. Longer periods require stronger and more durable competitive advantages.

## Stable growth consistency
- `g` must be sustainable indefinitely and should generally not exceed the nominal growth capacity of the economy/currency.
- Risk-free rate is a practical upper anchor.
- `Stable Reinvestment Rate = g / Stable ROC`.
- Default competitive-fade assumption: `ROC -> WACC`.
- Allow `ROC > WACC` only when durable barriers/advantages are explicitly supported.
- A stable `ROC < WACC` forever implies perpetual value destruction and requires explicit justification.

## Value of growth
Separate assets-in-place value from growth value where useful:
- Assets in place approximates normalized current operating earnings capitalized at a mature cost of capital under no excess-growth assumption.
- Growth value is the residual between total operating-asset value and assets-in-place value.

Growth is valuable only when returns on incremental capital exceed the opportunity cost of capital.
